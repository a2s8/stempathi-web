How STEMpathi teaches math

Updated 2026-11-15

Short answer: children move real-looking pieces first, then see the same idea as a picture, then as numbers. A balance scale shows what the equal sign means. The islands follow a learning path from first counting to equations.

STEMpathi at a glance

What it is
A hands-on math app. Children move cubes, tens and hundreds on a balance scale and see why each step works.
Ages
Made for ages 5 to 11, from first counting (Pre-K–K) to equations (grades 5–7).
What it teaches
Counting, more and fewer, making ten, tens and ones, hundreds, adding and taking away with place value, equal groups, and equations with an unknown x.

1. Concrete, then picture, then number

Each new idea starts with pieces a child can move: cubes, bars of ten, flats of a hundred. Next the same idea appears as a picture, and only then as digits and symbols. This order follows Bruner's enactive, iconic and symbolic modes[1], often called concrete–pictorial–abstract. A meta-analysis of manipulatives in math found a small-to-moderate benefit over symbols alone, and noted that how the pieces are used matters[3].

In STEMpathi the number on screen always matches the pieces. Ten loose cubes snap into one bar of ten, so "10" is something a child built.

2. A balance scale for the equal sign

Many children read "=" as "the answer comes next". Research links a clear sense of the equal sign as "both sides have the same value" to success with equations[4]. STEMpathi makes that meaning physical: every equation is a balance, and whatever you do to one pan you do to the other.

The balance model has limits, for example with negative numbers[5]. STEMpathi shows negative values as balloons that pull a pan up, so a positive cube and a negative balloon cancel, and the model keeps working.

3. A learning path, not a pile of drills

The journey is a row of islands in learning order. Each island builds on the one before, in the spirit of the learning-trajectories approach of Clements and Sarama[2]: a goal, a sequence of steps that most children pass through, and activities for each step. Our sequence is our own design, informed by that work and by the standards below; it is not an endorsement by the authors.

4. Calm by design

No timers to beat, no points race, no ads. Avi, the guide, offers a hint when a child is stuck. A grown-up can turn on calm motion and night mode.

What we do not claim

We do not claim that STEMpathi raises test scores; we have not run a study. We describe the method and the research it draws on, so parents and teachers can judge for themselves.
