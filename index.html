Why we built it

Some children need to hold a number before they can write it.

Many children are quick and curious, then freeze when a number is only a mark on a page. That is true of many children who find math hard, including many with dyscalculia, ADHD or autism. Give the same child five cubes to hold, move and count, and the idea starts to make sense. STEMpathi is built on that one idea: objects first, then pictures, then the written sum. Teachers call it concrete, pictorial, abstract. Children move cubes, tens and balloons on a balance, and the number on screen always matches the pieces. For parents, it means hands-on math practice for children who learn differently, at their own pace.

What the research says

  • A 2021 practice guide from the Institute of Education Sciences (US Department of Education) recommends concrete and semi-concrete representations for students who struggle with math. IES practice guide
  • A review of 55 studies with 7,237 learners found that teaching with objects beat abstract symbols alone, with small to moderate effects. Carbonneau, Marley & Selig, 2013
  • A 2018 review classed the concrete, pictures, symbols sequence as an evidence-based practice for students with learning disabilities who struggle with computation, such as adding and subtracting with regrouping. Bouck, Satsangi & Park, 2018

STEMpathi is a practice app, not a treatment or an assessment.

Questions parents ask

Before you install

What ages is it for?

It is made for ages 5 to 11. It starts with first counting (Pre-K–K) and goes on to equations (grades 5–7), so a family can use it for several years.

Is STEMpathi free?

It is free to download and free to start. All of kindergarten and the first two rounds of every later island are free. One optional family unlock opens everything else. It is a single purchase through Google Play, not a subscription, and Google Play shows the price in your currency. There are no ads.

Can my child buy something by accident?

No. The child never sees a price or a buy button. When a child reaches a locked island after the free rounds, the app says to ask a grown-up. The unlock is in the grown-ups area, behind a grown-ups gate, and Google Play handles the payment.

How long is a session?

Each island is a short set of puzzles. Ten to fifteen minutes with a grown-up nearby is a good start, and the app has no timers or streak pressure.
