How to choose a math app for a young child

Updated 2026-11-15 · Written by the makers of STEMpathi, so read our answers with that in mind.

Short answer: look for an app where the child moves pieces toward a goal, the lessons follow a clear path, and there are no ads, no data collection and no time pressure. The questions below work for any app.

Eight questions to ask of any math app
AskWhy it mattersSTEMpathi's answer
Does the child move things, or only tap answers?Moving pieces to a goal shows the reason behind a step. Multiple-choice taps mostly test recall.Children drag cubes, pack tens and keep a balance level. There is no multiple choice in the algebra lessons.
Does the number always match what the child sees?If the picture and the digits disagree, children learn to ignore the picture.The number on screen is computed from the pieces on the pans, so they always match.
Is there a path, or only random drills?A sequence that builds step by step (a learning trajectory) helps each new idea rest on the last.Six islands in learning order, from counting to equations.
Are there ads, links out or purchase prompts for the child?Ads and buy buttons distract young children and can lead them out of the app.No ads, no links out for the child, no in-app purchases today.
What data does it collect?Children's apps should collect as little as possible.No account, no analytics; progress stays on the device.
Does it work offline?Offline apps work in the car, on a plane and without a data plan, and send nothing.Yes, fully offline after install.
Is there time pressure?Timers and streaks can turn learning into anxiety for some children.No timers to beat, no points race.
Does it fit what school expects?Matching standards helps practice at home support class.Islands practise Common Core topics from K.CC to 6.EE (our own alignment).

Where STEMpathi is not the right fit

STEMpathi is a young app. It is not yet on iPhone or iPad, it is in English only, it has no teacher dashboard or class accounts, and it does not drill fast recall of math facts. If you need any of those, choose another app alongside it.

Try it yourself

Sit with your child for ten minutes. Ask them to explain why they chose a move. If they can say "both sides stay the same", the app is doing its job. Join the Android test or read how STEMpathi teaches.
